# Partnership Proposal
## HomeGuarantee × Coalition for the Homeless Houston
*Presented to CFTH Leadership | June 2026*

---

### Opening

The Coalition for the Homeless Houston and its partners have done something no other city in America has matched: reduced homelessness by 63% over a decade through disciplined, Housing First practice. The 3,280 people still experiencing homelessness in Houston today are the hardest to house — not because the system failed, but because one bottleneck has proven resistant to everything The Way Home's teams have tried: getting private landlords to say yes.

This proposal is about solving that bottleneck — together.

HomeGuarantee is not proposing to replace what CFTH does. We are proposing to build the financial infrastructure that makes the landlord engagement work CFTH has spent years doing dramatically more effective. The Way Home found the people, built the relationships, and designed the system. We want to fund the risk that has been keeping landlords on the sidelines.

We are bringing this to CFTH first, before investors, before other partners, and before building anything — because this only works if it's built with you.

---

### The Problem We're Solving Together

Houston has 18,900 active Housing Choice Vouchers and 3,280 homeless people. One in four voucher holders cannot use their voucher because no landlord will accept them. Texas SB 267 makes this legal. The Way Home's landlord team works this problem every day, and finding willing landlords is getting harder, not easier.

The barrier is not goodwill — it's financial risk. Landlords fear property damage, missed rent, and the administrative complexity of the HCV program. These fears are often exaggerated, but they are real enough to produce a "no" regardless of the quality of the tenant or the strength of the support system behind them.

The solution is a financial guarantee: a backstop that removes the landlord's risk and replaces it with certainty. When a landlord knows that damage is covered up to $20,000, rent is deposited on the 1st regardless of when HHA settles, and one person handles all the paperwork — the calculus changes.

HomeGuarantee is that backstop. CFTH is the pipeline, the infrastructure, and the decade of credibility that makes it work.

### What We're Proposing

A formal partnership in which HomeGuarantee and CFTH jointly operate a landlord guarantee and housing matching program for Houston, with the following structure:

#### What HomeGuarantee brings

**The guarantee fund.** A $2M capitalized reserve fund covering landlord losses — property damage, unpaid rent (up to 3 months), vacancy loss, and legal fees — up to $20,000 per tenancy. HomeGuarantee raises and manages this fund.

**The administrative lift.** A landlord experience layer: direct deposit rent on the 1st of every month; a dedicated liaison who handles all HUD inspection scheduling, HHA paperwork, and compliance requirements on the landlord's behalf. The goal is to make participating in HomeGuarantee easier than renting to a market tenant.

**The technology platform (Phase 2).** A matching and tenancy management platform that connects case managers to available landlord units, automates placement workflows, and tracks tenancy health so interventions happen before crises.

**The capital and the risk.** HomeGuarantee absorbs the financial risk of the guarantee fund. If claims come in, we pay them. CFTH carries no financial exposure.

#### What CFTH brings

**The coordinated entry pipeline.** Access to The Way Home's HMIS-enrolled individuals who have been prioritized for housing, with case management already in place. HomeGuarantee cannot source tenants independently — the coordinated entry system is how we know clients have support behind them.

**The landlord relationships.** Introductions to landlords already known to The Way Home's team, and co-presentation of HomeGuarantee to landlords as a CFTH-endorsed program. A landlord who trusts CFTH will trust the program faster than one approached cold by a startup.

**The case manager network.** Participation from The Way Home's partner organizations whose case managers will use HomeGuarantee to manage placements. Their active use is what makes the matching platform function.

**The institutional credibility.** CFTH's name, track record, and relationships with the City of Houston, HHA, and HCHA. These relationships will determine whether we can access CoC grant funds, HOME funds, and eventually HHA's platform fee budget.

### What CFTH Gets

We want to be explicit about this, because it matters. We are not asking CFTH to be a silent partner in exchange for the satisfaction of housing more people. CFTH should receive concrete, material benefits from this partnership proportional to the value it contributes.

We are proposing the following, open to negotiation:

#### 1. Revenue share on platform fees
When HomeGuarantee begins receiving platform fees from housing authorities (projected Phase 3, 12–18 months), CFTH receives 15% of gross platform fee revenue attributable to Houston placements. At 500 units and $87.50/unit/month, this is approximately $78,750/year — a meaningful supplemental income stream for CFTH operations.

This revenue share applies specifically to HomeGuarantee's Houston operations. If HomeGuarantee expands to other cities, those markets will have their own local partner arrangements negotiated separately. CFTH's 15% is not reduced if HomeGuarantee grows nationally — it is permanently tied to Houston placement volume, which CFTH's partnership directly enables.

#### 2. A seat on the HomeGuarantee Advisory Board
CFTH's Executive Director or a designee holds a permanent seat on HomeGuarantee's Advisory Board, with the right to review the guarantee fund's financial health, raise concerns about program direction, and have formal input into major product decisions. This is not a ceremonial role — it comes with quarterly reporting access and a genuine voice in how the program evolves.

#### 3. Co-branding on all Houston-facing materials
HomeGuarantee operates in Houston as "HomeGuarantee, in partnership with The Way Home." CFTH's logo and name appear on the landlord enrollment packet, the tenant-facing program materials, and all press releases. The credit for what gets built belongs to both organizations.

#### 4. Exclusive data and outcome reporting
CFTH receives a quarterly dashboard of HomeGuarantee placement outcomes: number housed, tenancy retention rates, claims data, and landlord participation by neighborhood. This data is owned by CFTH for its own reporting, grant applications, and advocacy work. HomeGuarantee does not publish Houston outcome data without CFTH's review and approval.

#### 5. Path to joint grant applications
HomeGuarantee will position CFTH as co-applicant (not sub-recipient) on federal grants where CFTH's participation strengthens the application — particularly HUD CoCBuilds funding and HOME Investment Partnerships. Revenue from joint grants is split in proportion to each organization's contribution to the application.

### What We Need From CFTH in Return

#### A signed partnership agreement before we enroll the first landlord

We are asking for a formal, written partnership agreement — not a letter of support, not a verbal commitment. The agreement should cover:

- CFTH's authorization for HomeGuarantee to identify itself as a CFTH partner in landlord outreach
- A data sharing framework for HMIS access (acknowledging HUD privacy requirements; we will work with CFTH's HMIS administrator to design the right approach)
- Case manager participation: The Way Home partner organizations are encouraged (not required) to use the HomeGuarantee platform for placements, with CFTH facilitating introductions to the network
- Introductions to the top 20 landlords already in relationship with The Way Home's landlord team

#### Honest feedback during the design phase

Before we build anything, we want to spend 30 days embedded with CFTH's landlord team and 3–5 case manager teams to understand what they actually do, where the bottlenecks are, and what a useful tool looks like. We will not build a platform that adds work to case managers' lives. We need CFTH to hold us accountable to that commitment.

#### A shared definition of success

We propose a joint steering committee — two representatives from CFTH, two from HomeGuarantee — that meets monthly during the pilot to review placement data, raise concerns, and make adjustments.

To keep the committee functional rather than a bottleneck, decisions fall into three categories with different approval requirements:

- **Operational decisions** (which landlords to recruit, which tenants to prioritize within coordinated entry guidelines, how to run a specific placement): HomeGuarantee's to make day-to-day without committee approval.
- **Program decisions** (adding new housing authority partners, changing guarantee coverage limits, modifying tenant eligibility criteria, neighborhood scoring): require joint committee approval.
- **Strategic decisions** (expanding to new cities, restructuring the revenue share, changing the co-branding arrangement, dissolving the partnership): require unanimous consent from both organizations' leadership.

Either party may pause the partnership with 90 days' written notice — extended from an initial draft of 60 days to give active tenancies time to transition. During the notice period, existing guarantee obligations remain in force and the rollback plan (see PRD Section 8a) activates.

### Why This Won't Work Without You — and Why We Think It Can Work With You

We want to be honest about something the pre-mortem on our own plan surfaced: HomeGuarantee, on its own, is a product with no distribution. We can build the guarantee fund. We can build the platform. We cannot source tenants from coordinated entry, build landlord trust overnight, or get case managers to change their workflows without CFTH's endorsement.

The partnership is not a nice-to-have. It is the product.

At the same time, we think there is something real here for CFTH. The Way Home has done the hard work of getting Houston to 3,280 — but the last mile is the hardest mile, and the landlord bottleneck has not yielded to the tools available. A financial guarantee, handled by a dedicated team whose only job is landlord success, removes the constraint that has limited how far The Way Home can go.

Together, the goal is achievable: Houston becomes the first major American city to functionally end chronic homelessness. That outcome, and the national attention it generates, belongs to The Way Home.

### Proposed Next Steps

1. **Initial meeting** — We would like to present this proposal in person to CFTH's Executive Director and relevant program leadership. We are requesting 60 minutes. We will come prepared with the full discovery work, financial model, and pre-mortem analysis.

2. **Mutual NDA + 30-day design sprint** — If CFTH is interested in proceeding, we sign a mutual non-disclosure agreement covering both parties before the sprint begins. HomeGuarantee will learn CFTH's operational workflows, landlord relationships, and system architecture during this period; CFTH will see HomeGuarantee's full model, financial projections, and product plans. Both sides deserve protection. The NDA is standard and mutual — not a negotiating tactic. Once signed, we spend 30 days embedded with CFTH's landlord team and 3–5 case manager teams. This lets both parties validate whether the partnership makes sense in practice, not just in principle, before any binding terms are agreed.

3. **Partnership agreement** — Following the design sprint, we would work with CFTH's legal counsel to draft a formal partnership agreement covering the terms above. We expect this to take 30–60 days.

4. **Joint funder approach** — With the partnership agreement in place, we approach CoC, HOME, and philanthropic funders together. CFTH's involvement significantly strengthens the grant applications that fund Phase 1.
