# Value Propositions: HomeGuarantee
**Date:** June 7, 2026
**Framework:** Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) — 6-part template: Who | Why | What before | How | What after | Alternatives
**Reference:** Stakeholder personas in `stakeholder-personas.md`

---

## Overview

HomeGuarantee has five distinct value propositions — one per stakeholder. Each uses the same JTBD structure but addresses a completely different job. The product only works if all five value propositions are simultaneously believable. A landlord who isn't convinced is a unit that doesn't get enrolled. A case manager who doesn't adopt is a tenant who doesn't get placed.

---

## VP-1 — Maria, The Small Landlord

*"My property earns what it should without the nightmares I've been warned about."*

### Who
Maria owns 2–8 rental units as a retirement investment. She is risk-averse, self-managing, and has either had a bad rental experience or heard enough stories to treat formerly homeless tenants as an automatic "no." She is not ideologically opposed — she is financially and operationally scared.

### Why (the functional + emotional job)
Maria needs to keep her properties generating reliable income so they remain an asset, not a liability, into retirement. Her fear isn't virtue — it's actuarial. She has seen what one bad tenancy does to her bottom line and her bandwidth. She needs to know that if she takes this risk, she won't regret it.

### What before (her life without HomeGuarantee)
- Cold-calls from housing agencies she doesn't know and doesn't trust
- No financial protection beyond a security deposit that doesn't cover real damage
- HUD inspection process she's done once and vowed never to repeat (7-week payment delay, paperwork she couldn't understand)
- Social fear: neighbors, her HOA, or other landlords judging her for "that kind of tenant"
- Every bad outcome falls entirely on her — money, time, stress, repairs

### How HomeGuarantee helps
- **Financial**: Up to $20,000 per tenancy guarantee — 4× any government program's ceiling. Claims paid within 30 days via ACH, no legal waiver required.
- **Operational**: A named HomeGuarantee liaison handles every piece of HUD paperwork. Rent lands in her bank account on the 1st of every month — not when HHA processes.
- **Social** (Phase 2.5): Pre-move-in neighbor introduction by a HomeGuarantee Ambassador. HOA pre-clearance before enrollment. "Certified HomeGuarantee Property" badge that reframes the narrative.
- **Managed tier** (Phase 2.5): Full-service option — she never fields a maintenance call. Quarterly property condition report. Monthly plain-English summary email. Zero required action.

### What after (her life with HomeGuarantee)
Maria's unit is occupied. Rent arrives the 1st of every month, every month. She has not received a single piece of HUD paperwork. Her neighbor asked her about the "certified program" badge on the door and she explained it with pride. When she had a damage claim in month 7, it was paid in 18 days. She has since enrolled a second unit.

### Alternatives (what she does if HomeGuarantee doesn't exist)
- Continues to screen out voucher holders and formerly homeless individuals
- Fills units with market-rate tenants at potentially lower rent (in a soft market) or leaves units vacant rather than "take the risk"
- Participates in a government program (Oregon/WA model) but with $5,000 max coverage — and no operational support

### One-sentence value proposition
**"HomeGuarantee covers up to $20,000 per tenancy and handles every piece of HUD paperwork — so you get reliable rent on the 1st and zero administrative surprises, guaranteed."**

## VP-2 — DeShawn, The Case Manager

*"I can focus on my clients instead of chasing landlords."*

### Who
DeShawn is a housing case manager at a Way Home nonprofit partner organization, managing 25–30 clients at a time and closing 3 placements per month. He is overworked and undersupported. He chose this job because he wants to help people — and the system's bureaucracy is getting in the way.

### Why (the functional + emotional job)
DeShawn's functional job is to place clients into stable housing as fast as possible. His emotional job is to feel like he's winning — to experience the moment when a client gets keys and their life changes. The system currently makes him feel like a cold-caller and a paper pusher. He needs tools that let him do the human work he is good at.

### What before (his life without HomeGuarantee)
- 10 cold calls to find 1 willing landlord. Most don't return calls.
- When he finds a landlord, HUD inspection delays kill the deal 40% of the time.
- Criminal backgrounds, prior evictions, and credit issues cause automatic screening rejections from landlord portals.
- He tracks placements in spreadsheets or memory. No shared visibility with his supervisor.
- Time-to-placement: 47 days on average. His clients are in crisis the entire time.

### How HomeGuarantee helps
- **Pipeline**: A searchable list of landlords who have already said yes — filterable by bedroom count, neighborhood, and FMR compatibility. No more cold calls.
- **Speed**: Inspections coordinated by HomeGuarantee. Lease signing triggered through the platform. Target: 21 days from match to keys.
- **Screening**: The guarantee replaces the credit/background screen as the landlord's risk management. Clients who would be auto-rejected can now be placed.
- **Platform** (Phase 2): Mobile-friendly intake form under 5 minutes. Placement tracking dashboard. Tenancy health alerts that tell him when a client needs attention before crisis hits.
- **Recognition**: When a client moves in, the platform sends DeShawn a notification with his name on it.

### What after (his life with HomeGuarantee)
DeShawn closed 7 placements last month — his personal record. He didn't make a single cold call. Two clients moved in within 14 days of being matched. His dashboard shows all 28 active tenancies in green. One tenancy flagged a health alert at day 58 — he called the client before the landlord ever knew there was a problem.

### Alternatives (what he does if HomeGuarantee doesn't exist)
- Continues cold-calling landlords and accepting a 3-placement-per-month ceiling
- Relies on The Way Home's existing (limited) landlord list, which doesn't grow fast enough
- Spends ~6 hours per placement on logistics he'd rather spend on clients

### One-sentence value proposition
**"HomeGuarantee gives case managers a pre-screened list of willing landlords, handles inspections automatically, and cuts time-to-placement from 47 days to 21 — so you spend your time on clients, not on paperwork."**

## VP-3 — Patricia, The Houston Housing Authority Director

*"My utilization numbers go up — and I have the data to prove it."*

### Who
Patricia is Deputy Director of Leasing at HHA, managing a team of 40 and reporting to the HHA board and HUD. HHA's voucher utilization rate is declining, the waitlist is frozen, and HUD performance reviews are looming. She has limited budget and high political sensitivity.

### Why (the functional + emotional job)
Patricia's functional job is to keep HHA's voucher utilization above HUD's performance threshold — without new money and without controversy. Her emotional job is to have a data exhibit she can walk into a HUD review with. She is not buying a product; she is buying political cover.

### What before (her life without HomeGuarantee)
- Voucher utilization rate declining (78% in 2025)
- HUD performance review at risk; board pressure increasing
- Landlord engagement team is underfunded and overextended
- Case managers across 100+ partner organizations are operating inconsistently with no shared platform
- Every time a voucher expires unused, it is a visible failure — and the cause (landlord refusal) is outside her control

### How HomeGuarantee helps
- **Utilization**: HomeGuarantee removes the primary cause of voucher non-use — landlord refusal. More enrolled landlords = more units available = fewer expired vouchers.
- **Data**: A dashboard formatted around HUD's own performance metrics (not HomeGuarantee's definitions). Patricia can screenshot it for her HUD annual review.
- **Speed**: Faster placement (21 days vs. 47) means vouchers don't expire before a unit is found.
- **Platform fee** (Phase 3): HHA pays $75–100/unit/month for a platform that demonstrably improves their utilization rate. ROI: $75/month buys a data exhibit that protects a $20M+ annual federal funding relationship.

### What after (her life with HomeGuarantee)
HHA's FY2026 HUD report shows 93% voucher utilization, up from 78%. 500 placements in the pilot year. 87% 12-month retention. The board asked Patricia to present it. The platform fee is a rounding error against the political and funding return.

### Alternatives (what she does if HomeGuarantee doesn't exist)
- Continues internal landlord engagement efforts with diminishing returns
- Accepts declining utilization and manages the HUD relationship defensively
- Applies for one-time landlord incentive grants (HUD signing bonus programs) that require ongoing re-application

### One-sentence value proposition
**"HomeGuarantee turns your voucher utilization rate into a HUD data exhibit — without new budget, by removing the one barrier you've never had a tool to solve: landlord refusal."**

## VP-4 — James, The CFTH Executive Director

*"Houston stays number one — and CFTH's name is in the headline."*

### Who
James is 61, a 14-year veteran of Houston's homelessness coalition work, and the gatekeeper to everything HomeGuarantee needs: the HMIS pipeline, 100+ partner organizations, and a decade of landlord relationships. He is motivated by legacy and by CFTH's organizational sustainability in an era of federal funding uncertainty.

### Why (the functional + emotional job)
James's functional job is to sustain Houston's Housing First progress and CFTH's central role in it — while diversifying revenue away from federal grant cycles that are increasingly unreliable. His emotional job is legacy: he wants Houston to be the city that ended chronic homelessness, and he wants CFTH's name on that story.

### What before (his life without HomeGuarantee)
- CFTH's landlord engagement team is reaching its limits; the remaining 3,280 people are the hardest to house
- Federal funding (12M of CFTH's $47M budget) is at risk from federal cuts
- No new revenue stream not tied to annual grant renewal
- The national recognition Houston deserves ("how Houston solved homelessness") hasn't arrived

### How HomeGuarantee helps
- **Mission**: HomeGuarantee fills the one gap CFTH cannot fill alone — private-market landlord enrollment at scale
- **Revenue**: 15% of HomeGuarantee's Houston platform fee revenue (scoped to the city, not eroded by national expansion), not tied to any grant cycle
- **Governance**: Board seat, co-branding (CFTH in the headline), joint grant applications. CFTH is a structural partner, not a referral source.
- **Rollback rights**: If HomeGuarantee ceases operations, CFTH has right of first refusal to assume all guarantee obligations using the remaining fund. CFTH's tenants are protected regardless of HomeGuarantee's fate.
- **Legacy**: HomeGuarantee's story is Houston's story. Every press placement names The Way Home coalition alongside HomeGuarantee.

### What after (his life with HomeGuarantee)
CFTH has a new revenue stream not dependent on federal appropriation. The 3,280 remaining homeless individuals are on a credible path to housing. The New York Times profile of Houston's homelessness success names The Way Home coalition in the headline. James's successor inherits a partnership that continues generating revenue and placements after his tenure.

### Alternatives (what he does if HomeGuarantee doesn't exist)
- CFTH continues doing what it does, but the landlord bottleneck doesn't move
- CFTH applies for additional grant funding for landlord engagement — competitive, one-time, unreliable
- Another organization (potentially less aligned) fills the private guarantee role without CFTH partnership

### One-sentence value proposition
**"HomeGuarantee expands what The Way Home can accomplish — with CFTH's name on the work, a revenue stream that survives any federal budget, and contractual protections for every tenant CFTH places."**

## VP-5 — Marcus, The Formerly Homeless Individual

*"Someone gave me a chance, and the system caught me when I almost fell."*

### Who
Marcus is 47, chronically homeless for 3.5 years, has a prior eviction and misdemeanor conviction, and has been rejected from housing 4 times because of standard tenant screening. He has been stable on mental health medication for 8 months — the longest stable period in years. He has the will. He has the support system. What he doesn't have is a landlord willing to take a chance on him.

### Why (the functional + emotional job)
Marcus's functional job is simple: a place with a door that locks, where he can sleep safely. His emotional job is dignity — to be treated as a prospective tenant, not as a risk to be managed, and to have a realistic chance at a life that isn't organized entirely around the daily crisis of not having a home.

### What before (his life without HomeGuarantee)
- Automatic rejection from every standard screening portal
- 4 prior placement attempts, 4 rejections, each one a blow to his belief that housing is actually possible
- Case managers who work hard but face structural barriers outside their control
- Moving into housing is a moment — but day 60–90 is when things typically fall apart, and no one is watching

### How HomeGuarantee helps
- **Access**: The guarantee removes the financial barrier that causes landlords to screen Marcus out. His prior eviction and record are no longer automatic disqualifiers — because the landlord's risk is covered.
- **Stability**: Tenancy health alerts flag his tenancy to his case manager if anything looks at risk — before a crisis happens, not after an eviction notice.
- **Dignity**: He is introduced as a tenant in a certified program, not as a formerly homeless person being placed in a landlord's unit.
- **Employment** (Phase 2.5 / Works): If Marcus has transferable skills — maintenance, landscaping, coordination — there may be a Works role that gives him employment connected to the mission that housed him.

### What after (his life with HomeGuarantee)
13 months in his apartment. Rent paid every month via his case manager's support. Knows his neighbor's name. Not thinking about where he's sleeping tomorrow. Has a Works job interview next week — his case manager referred him.

### Alternatives (what he does if HomeGuarantee doesn't exist)
- Continues cycling through shelters and placement attempts with no new outcome
- Relies on CFTH's existing placement efforts — which are already exhausting available willing landlords
- Eventually finds housing through a subsidized housing development (3–5 year waitlist)

### One-sentence value proposition
**"HomeGuarantee gives you a landlord who says yes — and a system that watches over your tenancy so a bad week at day 75 doesn't undo the housing you've worked for."**
