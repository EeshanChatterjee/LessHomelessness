# Go-to-Market Plan: HomeGuarantee
**Date:** June 7, 2026
**Scope:** Houston pilot → Texas → US national expansion
**Built from:** PRD v1.4, competitor analysis, positioning, value propositions, market sizing

---

## GTM Summary

HomeGuarantee sells a landlord guarantee platform into a market where the primary competitor is inaction. The GTM strategy is built around three realities:

1. **Trust, not tech, drives adoption.** No landlord signs a guarantee agreement because of a clean product interface. They sign because someone they trust vouched for the program, because the financial math is clear, and because the first 3 claims got paid quickly.

2. **The beachhead is a person, not a segment.** The first 50 enrolled landlords are recruited one at a time, by relationship, through the Houston Apartment Association and CFTH's existing landlord network. Scale follows proof; proof follows relationships.

3. **This is a two-sided marketplace.** Landlord supply without tenant demand is worthless, and vice versa. GTM must build both sides simultaneously — CFTH provides the demand (tenant pipeline); HomeGuarantee builds the supply (enrolled landlords). Neither side is a default.

## Phase 1 GTM: Houston Beachhead (Months 1–12)

### Goal
- 200 enrolled landlords
- 500 placed tenants
- First claims data (validates actuarial model for Phase 2 and insurance)
- Revenue: grant-funded (not commercial revenue in Phase 1)

### Beachhead Segment

**Primary: Small landlords (1–10 units) in Houston with one or more currently vacant units, connected to HAA or a real estate investor association**

These landlords are the most persuadable segment because:
- They make solo decisions (no committee, no board)
- They feel the vacancy pain acutely (every month a unit is empty is lost income)
- They have professional associations that provide warm introduction channels
- They are the segment with no existing landlord guarantee option (TheGuarantors targets large multifamily only)

**Secondary early segment: Absentee Houston landlords** (own Houston property, live elsewhere — cannot manage remotely without a partner). Ideal for the Managed tier in Phase 2.5. Identify through HCAD (Harris County Appraisal District) records — properties where owner mailing address is out of state.

### GTM Channels — Phase 1

#### Channel 1: Houston Apartment Association (HAA) — highest priority

HAA represents 900+ member companies managing 300,000+ Houston apartment units. More importantly, HAA has a culture of landlord education and peer influence.

**Tactic:**
- Request speaking slot at HAA Monthly Luncheon (largest regular landlord gathering in Houston; 200–400 attendees)
- Offer to present "The New Math on Housing Vouchers" — a data talk (not a sales pitch) showing the vacancy surplus, the FMR gap, and how the guarantee reprices the risk
- Negotiate HAA partnership: in exchange for access to members, HAA receives co-branding on any landlord education materials and a nominal revenue share per enrolled member (or recognition in the program)
- Build a standing relationship with HAA staff — a warm introduction from HAA staff to a member landlord converts 3–5× better than a cold approach

**Timeline:** Introductory meeting in Month 1; first speaking slot by Month 2

#### Channel 2: CFTH/The Way Home Landlord Referrals

CFTH's dedicated landlord engagement team has existing relationships with Houston landlords who have previously participated in voucher programs. Some of these landlords stopped because the administrative friction wasn't worth it — HomeGuarantee directly solves their stated reason for dropping out.

**Tactic:**
- Request a warm introduction list from CFTH's landlord team: specifically, landlords who participated in The Way Home programs in 2019–2023 and are no longer active. These are pre-qualified leads — they've already said yes once.
- Provide CFTH's landlord team with a co-branded one-pager they can share when a landlord asks "what's different now?"
- Offer CFTH landlord team a quota support tool — once the matching platform is live, their outreach directly feeds into the HomeGuarantee enrollment pipeline

**Timeline:** Month 1 (integrated into CFTH partnership agreement)

#### Channel 3: Landlord Peer Referral

The most powerful conversion moment is a landlord hearing from another landlord that claims got paid fast and the paperwork was handled. This is not a campaign — it is a designed moment.

**Tactic:**
- After the first claim is paid (target: Month 3–4), ask the landlord for a reference interview. Record a 90-second video with their permission.
- Build a private "founding landlord" channel (WhatsApp group or email list) for the first 50 enrolled landlords. Peer community creates social proof and reduces churn.
- Launch a formal referral program at Month 6: enrolled landlords earn a $250 credit (applied to platform fee) for each referred landlord who enrolls a unit.

**Conversion rate assumption:** Each enrolled landlord refers 0.5 new landlords on average. At 50 enrolled, that's 25 referrals — enough to hit 75 enrolled by Month 6 without additional outreach.

#### Channel 4: Direct Outreach (targeted, not mass)

Until the HAA relationship and peer referral flywheel are working, direct outreach fills the gap. This is the hardest channel — cold landlord outreach converts at <5% — and should be treated as a bridge, not a strategy.

**Tactic:**
- Build a target list from HCAD: single-family and small multifamily properties (2–8 units) with vacant units for 60+ days and out-of-state mailing addresses. These are the highest-pain landlords.
- Direct mail (physical letter) from a named person at HomeGuarantee. Landlords respond better to mail than email for financial products. Lead with the claim: "$20,000 guarantee. Rent on the 1st. We handle the paperwork."
- Follow up with phone outreach from the landlord liaison.

**Timeline:** Month 2–3 (while HAA relationship is being built)

- 200 enrolled landlords by Month 12
- Average landlord recruitment cost: <$500 per enrolled unit (including liaison time)
- 0 enrolled landlords who cancel within 90 days (signals mis-fit in enrollment)
- Landlord NPS ≥40 at 90 days
- ≥3 claims paid end-to-end by Month 6

## Phase 2 GTM: Houston Scale + Texas Prep (Months 13–24)

### Goal
- 500+ active placements in Houston
- HHA platform fee agreement signed
- Texas expansion: Austin, San Antonio, or Dallas as second market
- Revenue: Platform fee (HHA) begins; Managed tier revenue adds; Works begins earning

### Phase 2 GTM Changes

**Transition from relationship-led to platform-led acquisition.**

Phase 1 is built on relationships. Phase 2 is where the platform starts doing the acquisition work — enrolled landlords refer peers via the in-app referral program, case managers bring the platform to new partner organizations, and HHA's public endorsement (if achieved) legitimizes the program for skeptical landlords.

**Institutional buyer engagement: HHA**

Patricia's team has been tracking the pilot since Month 6. The Month 12 data package goes to HHA procurement with a platform fee proposal. The ask is structured as a 12-month pilot procurement agreement (not a multi-year contract) to reduce HHA's political risk.

HHA endorsement is the most powerful marketing asset for landlord recruitment: a signed partnership with the housing authority removes the single biggest landlord objection ("is this program real?").

**Managed tier launch (Month 9–12 bridge)**

The Managed tier is a new GTM motion — not just a different product tier. Managed landlords come from a different segment (absentee, HOA-community) and require different outreach:
- HCAD out-of-state landlord list (active outreach)
- Real estate investor forums (BiggerPockets Houston chapter, local investor meetups)
- HAA absentee member segment (HAA can filter its membership by location)

Target: 50 Managed enrollments by Month 18 (enough to begin Works self-funding runway).

### Texas Expansion Criteria

Before expanding to Dallas, Austin, or San Antonio:
- Houston has ≥500 active units
- Claims rate is ≤12% (validates insurability for the Texas model)
- Insurance reinsurance term sheet signed (reduces required fund capital for expansion)
- CFTH equivalent identified in the new market (local CoC with active HMIS and landlord engagement)

**Second market recommendation: Dallas-Fort Worth**

DFW has: ~40,000 homeless (PIT 2024), a functioning CoC (Metro Dallas Homeless Alliance), and a vacancy-to-homeless ratio well above 5×. The affordability gap (FMR vs. median asking) is comparable to Houston. Unlike Houston, DFW has no existing state landlord guarantee program to navigate. CFTH Houston can facilitate the introduction to Metro Dallas Homeless Alliance.

### National GTM motions

**Platform-led growth:** By Phase 3, the landlord matching platform creates a national network effect. A landlord in Chicago who sees Dallas's program performing well through peer networks creates organic expansion demand.

**Federal alignment:** If HUD's MTW evaluation (2026) recommends scaling landlord guarantee programs, HomeGuarantee positions as the implementation vehicle — not a competitor to be displaced. The policy brief and CFTH partnership are the groundwork for this.

**Insurance partner announcement:** Signing a reinsurance partner for Phase 3 is a press event, not just a product milestone. It legitimizes the platform to skeptical landlords nationally ("they've been around long enough that an insurer will back them").

**USICH relationship:** Engage USICH directly with HomeGuarantee's Houston pilot data. USICH's community profiles page is the most-read resource for homelessness program administrators nationally. Getting onto that page — as a case study, not just a citation — puts HomeGuarantee in front of every CoC director in the country.

## GTM Dependencies

The entire GTM plan depends on four things being true before Month 1 landlord outreach begins:

1. **CFTH partnership signed** — Without CFTH's warm introductions and tenant pipeline, HomeGuarantee is cold-calling landlords with no tenant to place.
2. **Guarantee fund capitalized** ($2M) — Without a capitalized fund, the guarantee is a promise, not a product. No landlord signs a guarantee agreement with an uncapitalized company.
3. **Kill-1 resolved** (TX insurance licensing) — HomeGuarantee cannot market a guarantee product to landlords until legal counsel confirms the product structure is compliant with Texas Insurance Code.
4. **Landlord Liaison hired** — The landlord enrollment process in Phase 1 is relationship-intensive. All channel activity flows through the Liaison. GTM cannot begin without this person in seat.

## GTM Narrative for Funders

The GTM story for philanthropic funders is not "we'll advertise." It is:

1. CFTH gives us the landlord introductions. Their 14 years of relationships is our distribution channel.
2. The HAA gives us the stage. Our speaking program reaches 400 landlords at a time.
3. The first claim paid is our marketing event. No ad, no campaign, no social post converts like "claim filed Monday, paid Friday."
4. Referral compounds. Each satisfied landlord is a sales person. At 0.5 referrals per enrolled landlord, 200 enrolled landlords becomes 300, then 450, without incremental spend.

This is not a paid acquisition model. It is a trust-based, relationship-compounding model appropriate for a market where the buyer is a property owner making a 2-year financial commitment, not a SaaS subscriber clicking a trial button.
