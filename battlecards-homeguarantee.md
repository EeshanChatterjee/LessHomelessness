# Competitive Battlecards: HomeGuarantee
**Date:** June 7, 2026
**Scope:** Sales-ready cards for landlord enrollment conversations and housing authority procurement discussions
**Built from:** Competitor analysis + positioning + value propositions

## How to Use These Battlecards

Each card is built for a specific conversation type. The landlord cards are for the HomeGuarantee landlord liaison in a 1:1 enrollment conversation. The housing authority card is for a procurement discovery meeting. The "Why HomeGuarantee" card is a universal closer.

**Rule:** Never lead with the competitor's name. Lead with the problem HomeGuarantee solves. Only name the alternative if the prospect raises it first.

## Battlecard 1 — vs. "I Already Know About Section 8 / HUD Programs"

*Use when: Landlord has heard of HCV/Section 8 and has had a bad experience with government programs*

### What they're really saying
"I tried this before and it wasn't worth the hassle. What makes you different?"

### The comparison they're making (implicitly)
Standard HCV enrollment: $750–$1,500 signing bonus, no damage protection beyond security deposit, 7-week payment delays, their own paperwork.

### HomeGuarantee's answer

| What they remember about HCV | What HomeGuarantee does instead |
|------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Rent payment delayed for weeks | Rent deposited the 1st of the month, every month — we advance it while HHA processes |
| Inspection paperwork was confusing | We schedule and coordinate the inspection; you don't interact with HHA at all |
| No real financial protection | Up to $20,000 guarantee per tenancy — paid within 30 days |
| One-time bonus with no ongoing relationship | Dedicated liaison on call; monthly summary email; claims support for the life of the tenancy |

### One-line closer
**"HCV is the voucher. We're the company that makes the voucher workable for landlords like you."**

### What to expect from them
- "How do I know the claim actually gets paid?" → Show them the claims governance (30-day ACH, no legal waiver required). If possible, offer a reference call with an enrolled landlord who has filed a claim.
- "What if the program shuts down?" → Explain the guarantee fund governance, the secondary committed capital, and CFTH as the successor — the guarantee runs out the lease period, not just while HomeGuarantee operates.

## Battlecard 2 — vs. Oregon Housing Choice Landlord Guarantee Program

*Use when: Prospect is in Oregon or references Oregon's program as the standard*

### What they know
Oregon's HCLGP is the oldest and best-known landlord guarantee program in the US. $5,000 coverage. State-administered. Free to landlords.

### The honest framing
Oregon's program is the predecessor. HomeGuarantee is the version that actually covers what can go wrong.

### Head-to-head

| Factor | Oregon HCLGP | HomeGuarantee |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| Coverage limit | **$5,000** | **$20,000** |
| Coverage per dollar of damage | 25 cents on the dollar above $5K | Full coverage to $20K |
| Admin support | None — landlord navigates HUA independently | Full liaison, inspection coordination, paperwork handled |
| Rent timing | Standard HUD payment schedule | 1st of month, guaranteed |
| Platform / matching | None | Case manager dashboard, landlord portal, HMIS integration |
| Funding reliability | State budget appropriation — can run out mid-year | Private capitalized fund with secondary committed capital |
| Managed property option | None | $175–225/unit/month full-service tier |
| Employment for tenants | None | HomeGuarantee Works program |
| Monthly fee to landlord | None | $87.50/unit/month (basic) — offset by value of admin support and rent guarantee |

### What to say about the fee
"Oregon's program is free to landlords. We charge $87.50/month. Here's why that math works: on a $1,450/month unit, our guarantee covers you for $20,000 — Oregon covers $5,000. That's a $15,000 difference in protection for $87.50/month. And we handle every piece of HUD paperwork, so you're not spending your own time. Most landlords find the fee pays for itself in the first maintenance request we coordinate."

### One-line closer
**"Oregon's program proved the concept. We built the product."**

## Battlecard 3 — vs. Washington State Landlord Mitigation Program

*Use when: Prospect has heard of WA's program or is in a state with a comparable program*

### What they know
Washington State's program: up to $5,000 damage reimbursement + $1,000 for move-in upgrades + 14 days rent loss. Free to landlords.

### The one fact that changes the conversation
**"Washington's program requires you to sign away your right to sue the tenant when you accept payment."**

When a landlord accepts WA's damage reimbursement, they contractually forfeit any further legal action against the tenant for the same damages. If the real damage is $12,000 and WA pays $5,000, the landlord collects $5,000 and can never seek the remaining $7,000 in court.

HomeGuarantee pays the claim **and** the landlord retains all legal rights.

### Head-to-head

| Factor | WA Landlord Mitigation Program | HomeGuarantee |
|--------|-------------------------------|---------------|
| Coverage limit | $5,000 damage + $1,000 upgrade | $20,000 total |
| Legal rights post-claim | **Forfeited — no further action** | **Retained — full legal recourse** |
| Admin support | None | Full liaison, paperwork, inspection |
| Platform | None | Full matching + claims platform |
| Rent timing | Standard HA timeline | 1st of month guaranteed |

### One-line closer
**"WA's program pays you $5,000 and takes away your right to do anything else. We pay up to $20,000 and you keep every right you had."**

## Battlecard 4 — vs. "I'll Just Use a Property Manager"

*Use when: Landlord says they already use or plan to use a local property management company for their units*

### What they're thinking
"I already pay someone to manage my property. Why do I need HomeGuarantee on top of that?"

### The honest answer
A property manager handles maintenance and tenant relations. They do not underwrite your financial risk from a formerly homeless tenant. They will not pay your claim. They will not handle HUD inspection paperwork. And they will almost certainly screen out any tenant with a prior eviction or criminal record — which means your unit stays vacant.

### What property managers do vs. HomeGuarantee

| Task | Property manager | HomeGuarantee |
|------|-----------------|---------------|
| Maintenance coordination | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes (Managed tier) |
| Tenant screening | ✅ Yes (screens OUT voucher holders) | ✅ Yes (guarantee REPLACES the screen) |
| Financial risk coverage | ❌ No | ✅ $20,000 per tenancy |
| HUD inspection coordination | ❌ Usually not | ✅ Yes |
| Monthly rent guarantee | ❌ No | ✅ 1st of month |
| Claims management | ❌ No | ✅ 30-day ACH payment |
| Access to HMIS tenant pipeline | ❌ No | ✅ Via CFTH partnership |

### If they have a property manager and want to keep them
HomeGuarantee Managed can coordinate with an existing PM on maintenance. The guarantee product and landlord experience layer work independently. Frame it as: "Your PM handles the building. We handle the risk."

### One-line closer
**"A property manager manages your property. We guarantee your income and take the risk off the table."**

## Battlecard 5 — vs. "I'll Wait and See" (No-Decision / Status Quo)

*Use when: Landlord seems interested but hedges with "I need to think about it" or "maybe later"*

### What's really happening
Inaction is the competition. For most landlords, not enrolling means nothing bad happens — their unit rents to a market tenant and the status quo continues. HomeGuarantee needs to make the cost of inaction visible.

### Reframe the status quo cost

**"If your unit is renting at market, you're probably fine. What's your vacancy rate?"**

Every month a unit sits vacant costs a landlord their full monthly rent. A 2-week vacancy between tenancies on a $1,450/month unit costs $725. HomeGuarantee's basic fee ($87.50/month) plus a reliable tenant who stays longer (the evidence from Oregon/CFTH shows 90%+ retention at 2 years) often yields better net economics than a high-turnover market-rate strategy.

For landlords with HOA exposure or neighborhood sensitivity concerns: "The longer your unit is vacant, the more your neighbors notice. A certified, supported tenancy with a program badge on the door is less visible pressure than an empty unit."

### For the "I want to see how it goes first" landlord
"That's exactly what we're doing. We're enrolling a founding cohort of 50 units in Houston. These are the landlords who will be the first claim paid, the first reference, and the ones who will tell other landlords in their building association whether this works. The founding cohort gets more attention from our team than any future enrollment will. If you want to wait until we have 200 units, you'll hear from your peers first — but you'll also enroll on worse terms."

### One-line closer
**"Waiting has a cost. Every vacant month is money not earned. Let's talk about what enrollment actually looks like."**

## Battlecard 6 — Housing Authority Procurement Card

*Use when: In a discovery or procurement meeting with HHA/HCHA or a comparable housing authority*

### Their primary concern
"How does this improve our voucher utilization rate, and when do we start paying for it?"

### The positioning
HomeGuarantee is not asking for a procurement commitment today. We are asking for a pilot data relationship and a commitment to a discovery call at Month 6 when we have results to show.

### What we bring to the meeting

**Month 0 (today):** A PRD, a CFTH partnership (or letter of intent), and a capitalized guarantee fund. We are not asking you to buy anything.

**Month 6:** Voucher utilization data from 50 active units. Claims rate. 90-day retention rate. Time-to-placement before and after. A dashboard formatted around your HUD performance metrics.

**Month 12+:** A platform fee proposal at $75–100/unit/month, with the pilot data as the ROI case. The fee is lower than the cost of HUD relationship management for the utilization problem we're solving.

### The metrics they care about (and how HomeGuarantee maps to them)

| HUD metric | HomeGuarantee contribution |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| Voucher utilization rate | Enrolled landlords = more unit options = fewer expired vouchers |
| Time-to-lease-up | 21-day placement target vs. 47-day average |
| Landlord participation rate | Growing enrolled landlord pool; direct outreach and referral program |
| Tenant retention at 12 months | ≥85% target; health alert system intervenes before crisis |

### Key objection: "We have our own landlord engagement team"
"We know, and we want to work with them. HomeGuarantee is not replacing your team — we're adding a financial product and a platform they can use. Your landlord liaisons spend time on outreach; we give them a tool that makes their outreach close more often."

### Key objection: "We can't pay a platform fee without budget"
"We agree — and we're not asking you to. The Phase 3 platform fee conversation happens at Month 6 with data in hand. Nothing we're asking today involves your budget."

### One-line closer
**"At Month 6, bring your voucher utilization director and your HUD performance data. We'll show you what happened to those metrics."**

## Universal Closer: "Why HomeGuarantee?"

*For any conversation — a 30-second explanation that works for every audience*

**What every other program has tried:** Government grants. Low coverage limits ($2,000–$5,000). No platform. Programs that depend on annual budget approval and can run out mid-year. Programs that ask landlords to waive their legal rights to collect payment.

**What HomeGuarantee does differently:**
1. $20,000 coverage — 4× the market ceiling
2. Handles the paperwork — landlords sign once and never talk to HHA again
3. Rent on the 1st — always, regardless of when HHA processes
4. Private funding — no annual grant cycle risk
5. Works — the people we house can become the people who run the program

**The case in one sentence:** "HomeGuarantee is the first private landlord guarantee that gives landlords a real reason to say yes — and the support to make it work after they do."
