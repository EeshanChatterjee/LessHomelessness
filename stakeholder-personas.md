# HomeGuarantee — Stakeholder Personas, Journey Maps & North Star Metrics
*Date: June 6, 2026*

> Five distinct stakeholders touch HomeGuarantee. Each has a fundamentally different job-to-be-done, a different definition of success, and a different failure mode. The product only works if all five experience value.

---

## Persona 1: Maria — The Small Landlord

**"I'm not a charity. But I'm not a villain either."**

### Profile
- **Age:** 52 | **Location:** Houston's Third Ward and East End
- **Portfolio:** 4 rental units (inherited 2, bought 2 as retirement investment)
- **Day job:** Medical billing administrator; property management is her side income
- **Experience:** 12 years as a landlord; one bad tenant experience involving $8,000 in damages shaped her screening behavior permanently
- **Current practice:** Runs a credit score minimum of 620, criminal background check, 3× rent income requirement. Explicitly refuses Section 8 because "the inspections take forever and HHA takes 6 weeks to pay."

### Primary Job-to-be-Done
Maintain predictable monthly income from her rental properties with the fewest possible surprises, so she can reach retirement without her properties becoming a liability. She is not optimizing for maximum rent — she is optimizing for minimum drama.

*"I just want the rent to show up and to not get a 2am call about a broken pipe that I have to pay for."*

### Top 3 Pain Points
1. **Financial unpredictability.** One non-paying tenant or one major damage event can wipe out 6–12 months of profit. She has no institutional safety net — she absorbs losses personally.
2. **HCV administrative burden.** She attempted Section 8 once. The inspection failed twice on minor issues (a light fixture, a window latch). HHA took 7 weeks to issue the first payment. She vowed never again.
3. **Screening stigma without data.** She knows she's probably screening out good tenants by using credit score as a proxy, but she doesn't know any other way to assess risk without spending hours she doesn't have.

### Top 3 Desired Gains
1. **Rent on the 1st, every month, no exceptions.** Not the 3rd. Not "pending HHA processing." The 1st.
2. **Someone else handles the paperwork.** If there's a liaison who does the HUD stuff for her, she will consider it. If she has to navigate HHA herself, she won't.
3. **Financial backstop that actually pays out.** She's seen landlord "incentive" programs that promise reimbursement and then make you jump through 10 hoops and wait 6 months. She wants a guarantee that pays in 30 days or it's not a guarantee.

### Unexpected Insight
Maria's refusal of Section 8 is not ideological — it's actuarial. She has mentally priced the risk of a bad tenancy at roughly $8,000–$15,000 and has decided the HCV program's administrative benefit doesn't offset it. **The moment the guarantee exceeds that mental threshold AND the administrative friction disappears, her calculation changes.** She is not a hard "no" — she is a "not worth it yet." HomeGuarantee is a repricing event.

### Product Fit Assessment
HomeGuarantee addresses all three of her core pain points directly:
- Direct deposit rent on the 1st removes the payment uncertainty
- A landlord liaison removes the HCV friction
- The $20K guarantee exceeds her mental loss threshold

**Potential friction:** She will be deeply skeptical that the guarantee actually pays in 30 days. The first 3 claims HomeGuarantee processes — and publicizes — will be the conversion event for dozens of Marias. Her word-of-mouth to other landlords at the Houston Apartment Association is the highest-value marketing action in the program.

### North Star Metric (Maria's definition of success)
> *"My unit has been occupied for 8 months, rent hits my account every 1st, I've had zero HUD paperwork cross my desk, and when the tenant scratched the floors last month, I got a check for $1,200 in 18 days."*

**Her metric:** Time between enrollment and first claim paid. If it's under 30 days, she recruits 3 other landlords.

## Persona 2: DeShawn — The Case Manager

**"I got into this to help people, not to fill out forms."**

### Profile
- **Age:** 31 | **Employer:** A mid-sized nonprofit in The Way Home network
- **Caseload:** 28 active clients; theoretically should be 20
- **Education:** BSW; working on his MSW evenings
- **Experience:** 4 years in housing services; burned through two colleagues who quit last year
- **Current process:** Spends ~6 hours per placement — coordinated entry intake, landlord cold calls, HUD inspection coordination, lease signing documentation. Closes ~3 placements per month. 
- **Emotional state:** Genuinely cares about clients; growing frustrated that systemic barriers prevent him from doing the part of the job he signed up for

### Primary Job-to-be-Done
Place clients into stable housing as quickly as possible so each client stops being in crisis — and so DeShawn can move to the next person. His metric is placements per month; his emotional fuel is seeing a client get their keys.

*"Every week someone's still on my list is a week they're still outside. I take that home with me."*

### Top 3 Pain Points
1. **Landlord cold calling is demoralizing and ineffective.** He calls 10 landlords to get 1 appointment. Most hang up when he mentions "voucher." He has no list of landlords who have already said yes.
2. **HUD inspection delays kill deals he's already negotiated.** He's had 4 placements fall apart in the last year because the landlord got tired of waiting for inspection clearance and rented to someone else.
3. **Criminal records and prior evictions auto-reject his most vulnerable clients.** The clients who are hardest to place are hardest to place for a reason — and standard screening criteria will never say yes to them regardless of the quality of the case management support behind them.

### Top 3 Desired Gains
1. **A searchable list of landlords who have already said yes.** He doesn't want to cold call. He wants to open a list, filter by bedroom count and zip code, and call someone who is expecting him.
2. **The inspection handled by someone else.** Not improved — removed from his plate entirely.
3. **Faster time-to-keys.** His current average is 47 days from match to move-in. He wants 21.

### Unexpected Insight
DeShawn doesn't just want tools that work — **he wants to feel like he's winning.** The placement is a moment of victory he shares with his client. A tool that makes him more effective but that obscures his role — where the landlord thinks HomeGuarantee placed them, not DeShawn — would undercut his motivation. **The product needs to make case managers feel like heroes, not operators of a platform.** Co-branding the placement victory with the case manager (not just HomeGuarantee and CFTH) is a retention and adoption strategy.

### Product Fit Assessment
HomeGuarantee directly solves Pain Points 1 and 2 and partially addresses 3 (the guarantee makes landlords say yes to clients they'd otherwise reject). The matching platform's value to DeShawn is entirely dependent on whether it saves him time — if it adds a step vs. his current process, he won't use it.

**Potential friction:** If the Phase 1 manual workflow requires DeShawn to fill out a structured intake form for each client, he will find it burdensome. The form design (pre-populated from HMIS, mobile-friendly, under 5 minutes) is the difference between adoption and abandonment.

### North Star Metric (DeShawn's definition of success)
> *"I closed 7 placements this month. I didn't cold call a single landlord. Two clients moved in within 14 days of being matched."*

**His metric:** Placements per month per case manager. Current baseline: 3. Target: 7+.

## Persona 3: Patricia — The Housing Authority Director

**"My job is to hit numbers. Help me hit the numbers."**

### Profile
- **Age:** 57 | **Role:** Deputy Director of Leasing, Houston Housing Authority
- **Manages:** A team of 40 leasing specialists; reports to the HHA Executive Director
- **Responsibility:** Voucher utilization rate — HUD measures this; falling below 95% triggers corrective action
- **Current challenge:** HHA's utilization rate has been declining as rents outpace FMR and landlords refuse more vouchers. Waitlist frozen since December 2023.
- **Budget posture:** Austerity. She is actively looking for things to cut, not buy.

### Primary Job-to-be-Done
Maintain the HHA's voucher utilization rate above HUD's performance threshold, demonstrate improved outcomes to the HHA board, and avoid anything that generates political controversy — all without getting new budget.

*"I don't have a problem with innovation. I have a problem with things that don't work or that blow up in the press."*

### Top 3 Pain Points
1. **Vouchers expiring unused.** Every unused voucher is a family that didn't get housed and a utilization rate that drops. HUD notices. She is accountable.
2. **Landlord refusal she can't address structurally.** Texas SB 267 means she can't mandate acceptance. She can run landlord outreach events, but that's not moving the needle.
3. **Slow procurement process that makes her look bureaucratic.** Anything new requires board approval, legal review, and IT security assessment. She has seen startups approach with great ideas and disappear into procurement purgatory.

### Top 3 Desired Gains
1. **A higher utilization rate she can report to HUD.** That's the outcome that protects her program and justifies her budget. Everything else is secondary.
2. **Outcome data she can share with the HHA board.** Number of people housed, tenancy retention rate, time-to-placement. She needs a dashboard, not a verbal report.
3. **A partner who understands government procurement and doesn't need her to explain it.** Startups waste her time by not knowing how government works. She'll engage with HomeGuarantee the moment she can see it's been designed for her environment.

### Unexpected Insight
Patricia is not buying a product — **she is buying political cover.** If HomeGuarantee improves her utilization rate by 15 percentage points, what she's actually buying is the ability to walk into a HUD review and say "we piloted a private-market guarantee program and housed 500 formerly homeless individuals." The product is the mechanism; the political narrative is the value. HomeGuarantee should design its reporting dashboard around HUD's annual performance review metrics, not its own definitions of success.

### Product Fit Assessment
HomeGuarantee's Phase 3 value proposition (platform fee for improved utilization rate) is exactly right for Patricia — but it requires 6–12 months of pilot data before she can justify procurement. The PRD correctly moves her to Phase 3 as the paying customer. The risk: if someone above her in the org (the HHA Executive Director or board) has a bad experience with a related program, the procurement conversation stalls regardless of the data.

**Potential friction:** She needs to see the claims process in action before she'll sign. "We'll pay claims in 30 days" is a claim; she needs a case study or a reference from another housing authority director who has seen it happen.

### North Star Metric (Patricia's definition of success)
> *"My FY2026 HUD report shows a 93% voucher utilization rate, up from 78%. I have a data exhibit showing 500 formerly homeless placements with an 87% 12-month retention rate. The board asked me to present it at the annual meeting."*

**Her metric:** Voucher utilization rate (reported annually to HUD). HomeGuarantee should design its housing authority dashboard to surface this number prominently.

## Persona 4: James — The CFTH Executive Director

**"We built this system. I need to know this makes it stronger, not replaces it."**

### Profile
- **Age:** 61 | **Role:** Executive Director, Coalition for the Homeless Houston
- **Tenure:** 14 years at CFTH; has seen the system go from chaos to 63% reduction
- **Manages:** A staff of 35; stewards relationships with 100+ partner organizations; reports to a board of civic leaders
- **Recent concern:** Federal funding cuts have put $12M of CFTH's annual $47M budget at risk. Houston's progress is fragile.
- **Public persona:** Quoted regularly in the Houston Chronicle, invited to the White House twice on Housing First; deeply invested in Houston's national reputation

### Primary Job-to-be-Done
Sustain Houston's progress on homelessness — and CFTH's central role in that progress — while securing financial stability that doesn't depend entirely on federal grant cycles.

*"If Houston ends chronic homelessness, it needs to be because of what we built here over 14 years, not because someone parachuted in with a tech solution."*

### Top 3 Pain Points
1. **Federal funding uncertainty undermining a decade of work.** He's built something fragile on grants that can disappear. He needs revenue diversification without compromising mission.
2. **The landlord bottleneck is the one thing he can't fix with his existing tools.** His landlord team is dedicated but outgunned. He knows the financial guarantee model works — Oregon proved it. He just doesn't have the capital to run it.
3. **Credit attribution risk.** Every external partner that touches The Way Home system risks claiming credit for outcomes CFTH's infrastructure made possible. He's seen it happen before.

### Top 3 Desired Gains
1. **More landlords saying yes — measurably.** If HomeGuarantee can add 200 enrolled landlords in 12 months, that is a genuine capability multiplier for The Way Home.
2. **A revenue stream not dependent on annual grant renewal.** The 15% platform fee revenue share is meaningful — but more importantly, it's reliable and growing.
3. **National recognition for Houston's model, with CFTH's name on it.** He wants Houston to be what people say when they talk about ending homelessness. HomeGuarantee co-branded with The Way Home accelerates that narrative.

### Unexpected Insight
James is motivated by **legacy, not metrics.** He is 61. He is thinking about what Houston looks like when he steps down in 4–5 years. A product that helps him put "Houston ended chronic homelessness" in that legacy is worth far more than the 15% revenue share. **The proposal's framing — "the credit for what gets built belongs to both organizations" — is the most important line in the document.** It addresses his real motivation, which is not money.

### Product Fit Assessment
HomeGuarantee is a near-perfect structural fit for CFTH: it adds capability (financial guarantee) that CFTH can't build, without competing with CFTH's core function (coordinated entry, case management, advocacy). The risk is entirely relational — if James doesn't trust the founding team or feels that HomeGuarantee is extracting value rather than adding it, the partnership fails at the first steering committee meeting.

**Potential friction:** James will want to know what happens to the guarantee obligations if HomeGuarantee folds. The rollback plan (CFTH as primary successor) is the right answer — but it needs to be in the partnership agreement, not just the PRD.

### North Star Metric (James's definition of success)
> *"The New York Times runs a story: 'How Houston Became the First Major American City to End Chronic Homelessness.' The Way Home is in the headline. HomeGuarantee is in the third paragraph."*

**His metric:** Houston's PIT count — specifically chronic homelessness. If the number reaches zero before he retires, that's the outcome. HomeGuarantee contributes to it but does not own it.

## Persona 5: Marcus — The Formerly Homeless Individual

**"I've been told I'm getting housed before. I'll believe it when I have the key."**

### Profile
- **Age:** 47 | **Location:** Currently in emergency shelter, Houston's Near Northside
- **History:** Chronically homeless for 3.5 years after job loss (warehouse work) and a mental health crisis. Prior eviction (2019) and a 2017 misdemeanor conviction on record.
- **Supports:** Case manager through The Way Home; enrolled in coordinated entry; active on mental health medication for 8 months — longest stable period in years
- **Race:** African American (56% of Houston's homeless population)
- **Housing history:** Has been in 4 placements since 2020, all failed within 6 months — two due to landlord screening rejection, one due to lease violations, one due to domestic conflict with a roommate

### Primary Job-to-be-Done
Get into a place of his own where he can sleep safely, cook his own food, and stop spending every day managing the immediate crisis of homelessness — so he can start rebuilding the rest of his life.

*"I don't need a lot. I need a door that locks. A place where my stuff doesn't get stolen. Somewhere I can just... exist without asking permission."*

### Top 3 Pain Points
1. **Being rejected before he gets a chance to show who he is.** His criminal record and prior eviction appear on every background check. He's been rejected by 7 landlords in the last 18 months. Each rejection erodes his willingness to try again.
2. **The process is re-traumatizing.** Every step — HMIS intake, tenant readiness class, landlord interview — requires him to recount his story, justify his past, and perform competence for strangers. He's been doing this for years and the exhaustion is palpable.
3. **Instability of past placements has damaged his self-trust.** He has been housed and lost it before. Part of him believes he will lose it again. This shows up as passivity — he stops advocating for himself when things go wrong because he doesn't believe intervention will help.

### Top 3 Desired Gains
1. **A landlord who doesn't ask about his record at the door.** He doesn't need a landlord who loves him. He needs one who is willing to give him a chance because the downside risk has been removed for them.
2. **Someone who intervenes before a crisis, not after.** His prior tenancy failures could have been prevented with earlier intervention (a mediated conversation with a landlord, a mental health check-in before an episode escalated). He needs a proactive support structure, not just a case manager he calls in an emergency.
3. **Dignity in the process.** He wants to be treated like a prospective tenant, not a charity case or a risk to be managed. Small things matter: being introduced to the landlord by name, being given a copy of the lease to review.

### Unexpected Insight
Marcus's most important decision point is not the placement — **it's the moment 3 months after move-in when something goes wrong.** Every prior tenancy failure happened in the 60–120 day window when the excitement of new housing wore off and real-life stressors emerged (a missed medication refill, a conflict with a neighbor, an unexpected bill). HomeGuarantee's tenancy health alerts (which flag at-risk tenancies for case manager intervention) are not a product feature — **they are the difference between Marcus being housed at Year 1 and Marcus being back in shelter.** The guarantee makes the landlord say yes; the tenancy health monitoring keeps Marcus in.

### Product Fit Assessment
HomeGuarantee's direct impact on Marcus is mediated entirely through the landlord and case manager personas — he doesn't interact with the product directly. But two HomeGuarantee features are critical to his success:
1. **Guarantee removes landlord screening barrier** — his record no longer auto-rejects him
2. **Tenancy health alerts trigger early intervention** — the 60–120 day vulnerability window gets caught

The gap: the product does not address Marcus's process re-traumatization. Every step still requires him to recount his history. HomeGuarantee could reduce this by streamlining the landlord introduction (case manager pre-briefs the landlord; Marcus arrives as a prospective tenant, not a candidate being evaluated).

### North Star Metric (Marcus's definition of success)
> *"It's been 13 months. I paid my rent. I know my neighbor's name. I cooked dinner last night. I'm not thinking about where I'm sleeping tomorrow."*

**His metric:** 12-month tenancy retention. Not placement. Not 90-day check-in. 12 months of stable housing is when Marcus starts to believe he can stay.

## North Star Metrics by Stakeholder

| Stakeholder | Their North Star | How HomeGuarantee Tracks It |
|------------|----------------|----------------------------|
| **Maria (Landlord)** | "Rent on the 1st, claim paid in 30 days, zero paperwork" | Days to claim payment; NPS at 90 days |
| **DeShawn (Case Manager)** | "7 placements per month, 18-day avg time-to-keys" | Placements/case manager/month; time-to-placement |
| **Patricia (Housing Authority)** | "Voucher utilization rate at 93% in HUD report" | Utilization rate improvement (housing authority dashboard) |
| **James (CFTH Executive Dir.)** | "Houston ends chronic homelessness. The Way Home's name is on it." | Houston PIT count — chronic homelessness number |
| **Marcus (Formerly Homeless)** | "Still in my apartment after 12 months" | 12-month tenancy retention rate |

### HomeGuarantee's North Star: Placements per month
This is the single metric that moves all five stakeholders forward simultaneously:
- More placements → Maria fills her unit, earns rent
- More placements → DeShawn hits his number, feels like he's winning
- More placements → Patricia's utilization rate climbs
- More placements → James's PIT count falls
- More placements → Marcus gets a key

**The product's job is to increase the rate at which landlords say yes and deals close. Everything else flows from that.**

## Stakeholder Tension Map

Not all stakeholders want the same things at the same time. Knowing where tensions exist prevents the product from inadvertently optimizing for one at another's expense.

| Tension | Between | Nature of conflict | Resolution |
|---------|---------|-------------------|------------|
| Speed vs. process | DeShawn (wants 14 days) | Patricia's team needs inspection clearance | HomeGuarantee handles inspection; removes tension |
| Credit attribution | James (CFTH) vs. HomeGuarantee | Both want recognition for placements | Co-branding agreement; CFTH in headline, HG in third paragraph |
| Disclosure vs. dignity | Patricia (needs data) vs. Marcus (wants privacy) | HA needs outcome data; Marcus deserves anonymity | Anonymized aggregate reporting; no individual disclosure to HA |
| Risk tolerance | Maria (risk-averse) vs. Marcus (needs a chance) | Maria's caution screens out Marcus | Guarantee product rebalances the calculus — Maria's risk goes down, Marcus's access goes up |
| Pace | James (long-term legacy) vs. HA procurement | James wants national story now; Patricia moves at government pace | Phase 3 sequencing; HG builds the story with CFTH before HA signs |
