# Positioning Statements: HomeGuarantee
**Date:** June 7, 2026
**Framework:** Classic positioning template + audience-specific messaging
**Built from:** Competitor analysis + value propositions + PRD v1.4

## The Master Positioning Statement

> **For** private landlords with 1–20 rental units in Houston who are willing to earn reliable income but not willing to take unreimbursed financial risk — **HomeGuarantee** is the only landlord guarantee platform that combines a $20,000-per-tenancy financial guarantee with full administrative support and a managed property option, **unlike** government landlord incentive programs that cap coverage at $5,000, provide no operational support, and can run out of funding mid-year.

---

## How to Read This Document

There are three distinct audiences who need to hear a different version of HomeGuarantee's story:

1. **Landlords** — the supply side; must enroll units or there's nothing to offer
2. **Housing authorities / government** — the institutional buyer in Phase 3; must see ROI in their own metrics
3. **Funders / philanthropists** — must believe in the model before writing the first check

Each audience has different vocabulary, different fears, and different definitions of success. The positioning below is tailored to each.

## Audience 1: Landlords

### Primary positioning

**"The landlord guarantee that actually covers what goes wrong — not just the easy stuff."**

### Full positioning narrative

You've heard about programs that help landlords rent to Section 8 voucher holders. You've probably also heard: the coverage is low, the paperwork is on you, and the program might run out of money. None of that is wrong.

HomeGuarantee is different. Not because the concept is new — landlord guarantee programs have existed since 2014. Because the product is different:

- **$20,000 coverage per tenancy** — not $2,000, not $5,000. If something goes wrong, the actual dollar amount that makes you whole.
- **We handle the HUD paperwork** — your dedicated HomeGuarantee liaison does the inspection scheduling, the housing authority forms, and the compliance paperwork. You sign once at enrollment and you're done.
- **Rent on the 1st of the month** — guaranteed. Not "when the housing authority processes." When you expect it.
- **Claims paid in 30 days** — by ACH, directly to your account.
- **The program doesn't run out of funding** — HomeGuarantee is a private company, not a city grant program. Your guarantee doesn't disappear mid-tenancy.

For landlords who want even less involvement: **HomeGuarantee Managed** handles maintenance, quarterly property inspections, HOA interactions, and neighbor introductions — for a flat monthly fee. You own the unit. We run it.

### Key messages for landlords

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|---------|
| "What if they trash the place?" | Up to $20,000 coverage per tenancy — paid in 30 days. That covers real damage, not just a security deposit. |
| "I tried Section 8 once — the paperwork was a nightmare." | Your HomeGuarantee liaison handles every piece of HUD paperwork. You don't interact with the housing authority at all. |
| "What if the program runs out of money?" | HomeGuarantee is a private company with a capitalized guarantee fund, not a city grant that depends on annual budget approval. |
| "My HOA might have a problem with this." | We review your HOA's CC&Rs before enrollment and handle any complaints on your behalf. |
| "I don't want to deal with maintenance calls." | Managed tier: we handle maintenance, inspections, and the monthly property report. You receive a summary email and nothing else. |

### Brand voice for landlords

Direct. Specific. Numbers-forward. Landlords don't respond to mission language — they respond to dollars, timelines, and what they're protected against. Every landlord-facing communication should open with coverage amount and close with how fast they get paid.

## Audience 2: Housing Authorities & Government Partners

### Primary positioning

**"The platform that turns your voucher utilization rate from a liability into a data exhibit."**

### Full positioning narrative

Housing authorities know the problem: vouchers going unused because landlords won't accept them. The solutions available today — HUD signing bonuses, state landlord guarantee programs — have coverage limits too low to change landlord behavior and no platform to systematize the enrollment process.

HomeGuarantee solves both. But for Patricia and her peers, the product story isn't the guarantee. It's the output: a voucher utilization rate she can report to HUD.

**What HomeGuarantee delivers for housing authorities:**
- A growing pool of enrolled landlords, actively adding units to the voucher market
- A platform that reduces case manager time-per-placement from 47 days to 21 — which means fewer vouchers expire
- A dashboard formatted around HUD's own performance metrics (not HomeGuarantee's definitions), ready for the annual performance review
- In Phase 3: a platform fee model ($75–100/unit/month) that the housing authority pays only after the program has delivered measurable utilization improvements — proof before purchase

**The ROI calculation for housing authorities:**
At 500 vouchers placed per year through HomeGuarantee, the platform fee is $75–100/unit/month = ~$45,000–60,000/month, or ~$540,000–720,000/year. The alternative: leaving those vouchers unused while HUD monitors utilization and federal funding is at risk. The platform fee is a fraction of what unutilized vouchers cost in HUD relationship management, reputational risk, and foregone housing production.

### Key messages for housing authorities

| Concern | Response |
|---------|---------|
| "We've tried landlord outreach — it doesn't scale." | HomeGuarantee's platform systematizes what landlord liaisons do manually, and adds $20K coverage that makes more landlords say yes. |
| "How do we know the placements are quality?" | 90-day tenancy retention ≥85%, claims rate ≤12% — both tracked and reportable through the platform dashboard. |
| "We can't pay a platform fee before we see results." | Correct — and we're not asking you to. Phase 1–2 runs on philanthropic funding. HHA engagement begins at Month 6 with pilot data in hand. |
| "What's CFTH's role?" | CFTH is a structural partner — not a vendor. CFTH controls the HMIS pipeline and case management network. HomeGuarantee is the landlord-facing product layer. |

### Brand voice for housing authorities

Institutional. Data-forward. ROI-explicit. Patricia needs language she can use in a board meeting and a HUD report. Every communication should include a metric, a percentage, or a comparison to the status quo. Avoid mission language with this audience — use performance language.

## Audience 3: Funders & Philanthropists

### Primary positioning

**"The first landlord guarantee program designed to pay for itself — and employ the people it houses while doing it."**

### Full positioning narrative

For 10 years, landlord risk mitigation funds have been a promising idea trapped in a structural problem: they depend on annual grants, have coverage too low to change behavior at scale, and are designed to spend down, not to grow.

HomeGuarantee is a different structure. It starts with philanthropy — a $2M guarantee fund and $750K operating bridge — and builds toward a private-market revenue model in which housing authorities pay a platform fee, Managed tier landlords pay a monthly fee, and HomeGuarantee Works is funded by earned revenue from the landlords it serves.

**Why this matters for funders:**
- Your philanthropic dollar is used for a specific purpose: to de-risk the first 18 months of operations until the revenue model becomes self-sustaining. It is not perpetual subsidy.
- The guarantee fund doesn't get consumed — it is a reserve that transitions to insurance-backed coverage in Phase 3, at which point philanthropic capital is returned or redirected.
- The Works component employs formerly homeless individuals in the jobs that make the housing program possible. You are not funding a program that hands off employment to a separate organization — you are funding a circular economy in which every housed person is a potential Works employee and every Works employee enables more housing.

**The story for impact investors:**
HomeGuarantee is building a moat no government program can replicate: CFTH partnership (HMIS access, case manager relationships, 100+ partner orgs), a claims data flywheel (18–24 months of actuarial data → insurance reinsurance → lower capital requirement → scale), and the Works workforce model (purpose-aligned employment that reduces labor cost while generating press-worthy narrative and workforce-specific funding channels).

### Key messages for funders

| Concern | Response |
|---------|---------|
| "Is the guarantee fund at risk of depleting?" | The fund has a governance structure with depletion triggers, secondary committed capital, and a path to insurance-backed reinsurance in Phase 3. Full fund governance is in the PRD. |
| "What's the exit for philanthropy?" | Philanthropy seeds the fund and bridges operations. When the revenue model is self-sustaining (~Month 18) and insurance replaces the fund, philanthropic capital is returned or redirected to other housing programs. |
| "Why not just give the money to CFTH?" | CFTH doesn't have a private-market product that changes landlord behavior at scale. HomeGuarantee does what CFTH explicitly cannot: enroll private landlords with financial guarantees, not just case management relationships. |
| "What's the replication plan?" | Houston proves the model (Phase 1–2), Texas proves it at state scale (Phase 3), then Houston → Chicago → Phoenix → Atlanta → Denver. The platform, not the people, scales the model. |
| "Who's on the founding team?" | [Kill-5 — to be answered before sharing this document externally.] |

### Brand voice for funders

Systems-thinking. Mission-aligned but financially rigorous. Funders in this space have seen many passionate plans with weak financial models. HomeGuarantee needs to communicate both the mission (housing is a human right; the circular economy model is morally coherent) and the structure (kill switches, fund governance, phased revenue model, insurance transition). Lead with the structural discipline; let the mission land second.

### What HomeGuarantee is NOT positioned as

- A charity program (it has a revenue model)
- A government program (it is private, which is a feature — it doesn't run out of funding mid-year)
- A property management company (it enhances the landlord relationship; the Managed tier is opt-in, not mandatory)
- A tenant advocacy organization (it serves landlords; tenant outcomes are the *result* of serving landlords well)
- A technology company (the tech enables the guarantee; the guarantee is the product)
