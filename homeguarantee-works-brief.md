# HomeGuarantee Works
## A Circular Economy Model for Housing Stability and Employment
*Concept Brief for Funders and Partners | June 2026*

---

### The Idea in One Sentence

HomeGuarantee Works hires formerly homeless individuals — people whose housing was enabled by the HomeGuarantee program — to staff the property management and community liaison roles that make more housing placements possible.

---

### The Problem It Solves

HomeGuarantee's landlord guarantee product requires an operations layer to function at scale: someone has to coordinate maintenance, conduct quarterly property condition checks, introduce the program to neighbors, and field landlord questions at 11pm. That workforce needs to be:

- Locally embedded (understands Houston neighborhoods)
- Motivated (this work directly enables more housing)
- Affordable (labor cost is the primary constraint on Managed tier unit economics)
- Scalable (one FTE should cover 25–35 units)

At the same time, Houston's homeless service system has a persistent problem: people exit homelessness into unstable employment, cycle back into crisis, and re-enter the system. Employment is the bridge between stable housing and lasting stability — and it's the piece most housing programs hand off to someone else.

HomeGuarantee Works treats these two problems as the same problem.

---

### How It Works

#### Step 1 — Skills identification
CFTH's coordinated entry system and The Way Home's partner organizations identify individuals who are newly housed (or approaching stable housing) and who have relevant skills: construction trades, maintenance, landscaping, cleaning, property management, administrative coordination, or community outreach.

No minimum experience required — basic competency and reliability is the bar.

#### Step 2 — Training (≤8 weeks)
HomeGuarantee partners with existing Houston-area workforce development organizations (Interfaith Ministries, Houston Works, Star of Hope) for any needed skills training. HomeGuarantee does not build training programs from scratch — it acts as an employer that sources from existing pipelines.

Training tracks:
- **Property Condition Reporter** — Conducts quarterly walk-throughs with a standardized checklist and photo documentation. No prior experience required; 2-week training.
- **Maintenance Coordinator** — Schedules and oversees contractor work on behalf of landlords. Requires organizational and communication skills; 4-week training.
- **Neighborhood Ambassador** — Conducts pre-move-in introductions to neighbors, returns at 90 days, serves as a named contact for community questions. Requires interpersonal skills; 3-week training.
- **Trades Contractor (subcontract)** — Plumbers, electricians, handymen with existing licensure or apprenticeship placement. HomeGuarantee connects them to the trades network as certified subcontractors; no training required if licensed.

#### Step 3 — Employment
HomeGuarantee Works employees are direct employees of HomeGuarantee (not contractors or gig workers). Benefits include:
- Hourly wage ($18–22/hour based on role; above Houston living wage)
- Health coverage (employer-sponsored, partially subsidized by HomeGuarantee's grant funding)
- Case management support from The Way Home for the first 12 months of employment (existing CFTH infrastructure)
- A named supervisor at HomeGuarantee who is not their housing case manager (separation of roles prevents conflict of interest)

#### Step 4 — The Circular Loop
Works employees enable more landlord enrollments → more enrollments mean more placements → more placements mean more people who might qualify for Works employment → the workforce grows with the housing program.

At 500 enrolled units (HomeGuarantee's Year 1 target), the operation requires approximately:
- 5 Property Condition Reporters (100 units each per quarter)
- 3 Maintenance Coordinators (150–200 units each)
- 4 Neighborhood Ambassadors (based on placement volume, ~125 placements/year)
- ~15 Trades subcontractors (on-demand, not full-time employees)

= **12 direct Works employees** at Year 1. At 2,000 units (Year 2–3), approximately 48 direct employees.

---

### Why This Is Different

Every housing-to-employment program HomeGuarantee is aware of treats employment as a separate downstream activity — something that happens after stable housing is achieved, at a different organization, through a different program. Works collapses that handoff.

The employee's job is directly connected to the mission that housed them. They are not cleaning someone else's office building — they are maintaining the infrastructure that keeps their community housed. That is a fundamentally different employment relationship, and there is evidence from comparable programs (social enterprises run by formerly homeless people in Europe and in select US cities) that purpose-aligned employment produces significantly lower attrition rates than standard workforce placements.

For HomeGuarantee, Works is not a charity program — it is the most cost-effective source of the specific labor the Managed tier requires.

### The Funding Case

HomeGuarantee Works is fundable through multiple channels simultaneously:

| Funding source | Rationale | Estimated amount |
|---------------|-----------|-----------------|
| HUD Homeless Assistance Grants | Employment as a component of homeless services programs | $100K–$300K/year |
| Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA) | Subsidized wages for workers from targeted populations (homeless qualifies) | $50K–$150K/year |
| Texas Workforce Commission grants | Employing individuals from vulnerable populations | $50K–$100K/year |
| Corporate CRA (JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, Regions) | Community Reinvestment Act credit for workforce development serving LMI populations | $100K–$300K (one-time or annual) |
| Houston Endowment / Arnold Ventures | Systemic innovation in homelessness systems | $250K–$1M (program foundation grant) |
| Earned revenue (Managed tier) | Works employees are funded by the $175–225/unit/month fee; no subsidy needed at scale | Self-sustaining at ~300+ managed units |

**The path to self-funding:** At 300 actively managed units at $200/unit/month = $720K/month revenue. Works labor cost (12 FTEs at $55K fully loaded average): $660K/year = $55K/month. Works is covered by earned revenue at 300 units — well before HomeGuarantee's Year 2 milestone.

Until that point, Works is grant-funded. The grant ask is not "fund our charity" — it is "fund the bridge to a self-sustaining employment program." That framing has historically outperformed pure service grants in competitive federal and philanthropic funding.

---

### What Success Looks Like

**At 12 months:**
- 12 Works employees hired and retained
- 0 employees returned to homelessness
- Employee 90-day retention rate ≥85% (above industry average for workforce development programs)
- Average hourly wage ≥$18/hour
- Works employees enabled ≥400 property condition reports and ≥100 neighbor ambassador visits

**At 36 months:**
- 48+ Works employees
- First Works employees promoted to supervisor or senior roles
- 3–5 Works subcontractors have established their own licensed contracting businesses
- Program is fully self-funded through Managed tier earned revenue
- Model documented and shared with 3 peer cities considering HomeGuarantee expansion
