Persona 1

Marcus — The Risk-Averse Landlord

🏠
Marcus T.
Small landlord · 3 properties · S-Corp LLC · Houston Heights
HCV Skeptic → Converted Advocate
Background

Marcus owns 3 single-family rentals. He inherited one property, bought two as investment. His previous Section 8 experience ended badly — one tenant left $4,700 in damage, one inspection failed without warning. He's been HCV-free for 4 years. He's not a bad actor; he's burned. His current tenants pay $1,450/mo market rate but he worries every month about late payments and turnover costs.

"I don't hate the program. I hate what happened to me the last time I trusted it. Show me the backstop and I'll reconsider."
JTBD — Jobs to Be Done
Protect my cash flow from a single bad-actor tenant wiping out 3 months of profit
Know within 48 hours if a unit is at risk of damage or non-payment
Reduce time spent on property management — I have a day job
Access HCV demand without navigating HUD bureaucracy
Pain → Gain
HCV inspection process took 3 months + surprise failures
Pre-inspected unit acceptance — no repeated failures
Previous $4,700 damage claim took 8 months to resolve through HUD
30-day guarantee payout — no bureaucratic lag
No visibility into tenant risk before signing lease
Risk-scored tenant match through platform
Missed 47 days vacancy between last two tenants
3-month vacancy waiver covers downtime
Skeptic
Curious
Trials Basic
Enrolls 1 unit
Enrolls all 3
North Star Metric
Time-to-claim-resolution ≤ 30 days for 90% of claims
Secondary: % landlords who re-enroll after first claim paid
Persona 2

Patricia — The Housing Authority Director

🏛️
Patricia R.
Deputy Director, Houston Housing Authority · 20yr career · Former HUD policy analyst
System Builder · Policy Driver
Background

Patricia manages 18,900 active HCV vouchers. 4,725 (25%) go unused every year because landlords won't accept them. She's accountable to HUD on voucher utilization rates and has been flagged twice for under-performance. She's politically constrained by Texas SB 267 (legal source-of-income discrimination), so she can't mandate acceptance. She needs a market-based mechanism that makes HCV attractive without legislative change.

"I have $235M in voucher funds. I can't get landlords to take the vouchers. If you can solve that, I'll fund your guarantee reserve myself."
JTBD — Jobs to Be Done
Increase HCV utilization rate from 75% to 95% within 24 months
Demonstrate measurable homelessness reduction to HUD without new legislation
Reduce staff overhead of landlord outreach and onboarding (currently 2 FTE dedicated)
Build a data-auditable portfolio for HUD reporting
Pain → Gain
HUD flags HA when utilization drops below 95%
Platform surfaces landlord acceptance funnel; identifies blockages
2 FTE dedicated solely to landlord outreach with minimal results
Automated landlord onboarding flow via platform
SB 267 legally allows landlords to reject HCV — no enforcement mechanism
Market-based incentive removes need for legal enforcement
No real-time data on why vouchers go unused
Analytics dashboard shows rejection reasons by property type, zip code
North Star Metric
HCV utilization rate ≥ 95% within 24 months of platform go-live
Secondary: landlord onboarding cost per HA FTE ≤ $200/unit/year
Persona 3

Jerome — The Works Employee

👷
Jerome W.
Works crew member · 6 months employed · Previously chronically homeless 4 yrs · Third Ward
Economic Ladder · Workforce Participant
Background

Jerome was chronically homeless for 4 years after a back injury ended his HVAC career and led to opioid dependency and job loss. He entered Works via a CoC referral. He now earns $16.83/hr on renovation crews — the first steady income he's had in years. His SSI benefits were reduced when his earnings exceeded the threshold. He lives in a Works transitional unit (NOT a Stabilization unit). He's on a 24-month pathway to market-rate independent housing.

"I'm not a charity case. I'm a carpenter. I just needed someone to bet on me first."
JTBD — Jobs to Be Done
Earn stable income without triggering SSI clawback that leaves me worse off than unemployment
Build an employment record that qualifies me for market-rate rental applications
Develop trade skills (carpentry, HVAC basics, plumbing) that transfer to private sector
Transition from Works transitional housing to independent market-rate tenancy within 24 months
Pain → Gain
SSI reduces $1 for every $2 earned above $85 threshold — earning $600/mo can net less than $0
Works progression plan calibrates hours to avoid SSI cliff — Kill Switch K9 enforced
Background check + no rental history = automatic rejection at all private market listings
Works provides verifiable income + HomeGuarantee letter of support for rental applications
No path from "homeless" to "employed tenant" without co-locating SSI and wages (which creates clawback)
Works transitional units are separate from Stabilization units — no SSI offset loop
North Star Metric
% Works employees who achieve independent market-rate tenancy within 24 months
Target: 60% by Year 3. Secondary: 12-month Works retention rate ≥ 70%
Persona 4

Maria — The CoC Case Manager

🧑‍⚕️
Maria S.
Case Manager, The Way Home Houston CoC · 8yr role · Manages 35 active clients
Coordination Hub · HMIS Operator
Background

Maria manages 35 active clients across the HMIS system. She's the human glue of the housing system — she knows every landlord, every referral pathway, every waitlist. She's chronically understaffed (budget cuts in Year 2 reduced her team by 20%) and relies on a patchwork of spreadsheets, HMIS exports, and phone calls. She's been excited by HomeGuarantee because it promises to give her clients more landlord options — but she's seen too many "savior" platforms fail to stick.

"I don't need another app. I need landlords who will pick up my call on Friday afternoon when my client is about to sleep in a parking lot."
JTBD — Jobs to Be Done
Find landlords willing to accept HCV tenants in neighborhoods with transit access
Reduce time spent on manual landlord matching from 6 hours/placement to <2 hours
Get real-time data on which units are available — not a list that's 3 weeks stale
Document housing outcomes in HMIS without double-entry
Pain → Gain
Unit availability lists are always outdated — landlords don't update them
Real-time unit availability API via platform (integrated with HMIS)
Every placement requires 12+ landlord calls before finding one willing to accept HCV
HomeGuarantee pre-vetted landlord network — all are HCV-willing
HMIS entry is duplicated across housing authority, CoC, and service provider systems
Single-entry HMIS sync exports to HA and platform simultaneously
North Star Metric
Days from housing referral to signed lease ≤ 21 (down from 63 days baseline)
Secondary: Maria's active caseload she can handle without admin overflow
Persona 5

Devon — The HCV Holder

🧑‍🦽
Devon A.
HCV holder, 14 months · Disability SSDI · Former construction worker · East End Houston
Voucher Trap · Forced-to-Choose Tenant
Background

Devon received an HCV 14 months ago. His voucher expires in 5 months — he's used 9 of his 12-month search window (extended once already). He's called 47 landlords. 31 didn't return calls. 16 said yes initially, then rejected him after learning he had a voucher. He has SSDI income, no eviction history, and a clean background check. He is a perfect tenant by any objective measure. He just can't find a landlord who will take the voucher. He'll lose the voucher if he doesn't sign by Month 14.

"I've never been late on rent in my life. I don't understand why having government-backed guaranteed rent makes me less desirable."
JTBD — Jobs to Be Done
Find a 1BR unit in an area with bus access to his medical appointments before voucher expiry
Not have to explain or defend his voucher status during the application process
Stay housed long enough to stabilize his health without the stress of impending eviction or voucher loss
Pain → Gain
47 landlord rejections — almost all voucher-related despite clean record
HCV-accepting landlord network — guaranteed to accept vouchers
Voucher expiry in 5 months — searching 4 hours/day with no success
Maria matches Devon to a platform landlord in <21 days via CoC referral
SB 267 means landlords face zero legal consequences for HCV rejection
Financial incentive replaces legal mandate — landlords want Devon now
North Star Metric
% HCV holders housed before voucher expiry: target 90% (vs 75% baseline)
Secondary: % housed in areas with transit access
Persona 6 · Phase 2.5b · NEW

Ray — The Distressed Property Owner

⭐ This persona is the strategic unlock for Phase 2.5b. Ray is the supply constraint that no other persona can solve. Without Ray, the Stabilization tier doesn't exist.
🔑
Ray K.
Absentee owner · 2 properties · Both vacant 3+ years · One tax-delinquent · Cleveland, OH (owns Houston East End properties)
Phase 2.5b Supply Unlock · Below-FMR Tier
Background

Ray inherited two single-family homes in Houston's East End from his grandmother in 2019. He lives in Cleveland. One property is in acceptable condition but has sat vacant for 2 years — he can't find a tenant he trusts from 1,200 miles away and doesn't have the capital to hire a property manager. The second property is tax-delinquent ($14,000 owed to HCAD), has mold damage, failed electrical wiring, and is at risk of foreclosure within 18 months. Ray has received three letters from HCAD but doesn't know what to do. His maximum valuation scenario is that the properties are "worth something someday."

"I can't afford to fix it. I can't afford to sell it. And every month I don't do something, it gets worse. If someone wants to handle it and give me anything back, I'm listening."
Two Sub-types of Ray
Ray Type A — Active Carrier (Non-Delinquent): Property 1. Acceptable condition. Vacant 2 yrs. Can sign Renovation Exchange at full contract ($175). Works crew brings unit to habitability standard. Owner pays $175, commits to 18-month $200–350/mo lease. Net: turns a $0 asset into ~$280/mo positive cash flow.
Ray Type B — Delinquent (Salvage): Property 2. Tax-delinquent. Mold + electrical. At foreclosure risk. Renovation is a free concession — Ray commits to 18-month $200–350/mo lease; HomeGuarantee's Works crew does the renovation at no cost to Ray. Net: removes foreclosure threat, generates $280/mo. Works earns $14,400 in labor income only (no renovation contract fee).
JTBD — Jobs to Be Done
Stop the financial and emotional drain of a vacant property I can't sell or rent from afar
Avoid foreclosure on the delinquent property without having to move to Houston
Turn a liability into a monthly cash-flow asset with zero upfront capital outlay
Not have to deal with tenant management — I'm 1,200 miles away
HCAD Targeting Logic
🔎
HCAD (Harris County Appraisal District) provides public data on: vacant properties, tax delinquency status, absentee owner addresses, property condition flags. HomeGuarantee uses HCAD to identify Ray before Ray knows HomeGuarantee exists.
Pain → Gain
Property generates $0 income while accumulating $14K+ in back taxes
Renovation Exchange + 18-mo lease = ~$280/mo net positive cash flow from Day 1
Can't hire a property manager without enough capital to cover deposits
HomeGuarantee manages the property — Ray gets a check, not a call
Foreclosure in 18 months = total asset loss
Works crew renovates property; prevents foreclosure; creates rental income
Renovation cost to bring unit to habitability: $15,000–$40,000
Type B owners: renovation at zero upfront cost in exchange for lease commitment only
North Star Metric
Distressed units converted to Stabilization lease within 90 days of HCAD outreach
Target: 30% conversion of outreach → signed lease at 6-month pilot review. Kill Switch K8: if <15% at Month 6, pause Stab enrollment.
