# Discovery Plan: Landlord Non-Financial Risk Mitigation
## HomeGuarantee — Managed Property Layer + Acceptance Risk

**Date**: June 6, 2026
**Product stage**: Existing product extension (HomeGuarantee v2)
**Discovery question**: Beyond the financial guarantee, what would make a landlord genuinely *want* to participate — and keep participating?

---

## Context

The financial guarantee resolves Maria's primary objection. But the pre-mortem and persona work surfaced two unaddressed risks that no amount of money covers:

**Punitive/operational risk**: Even with the financial backstop, Maria still has to find a plumber at 11pm, field a call from a distressed tenant, coordinate HUD inspection re-visits, and deal with whatever situation the tenant creates that isn't a dollar loss but is a headache. The guarantee makes her whole financially; it doesn't make her life easier operationally.

**Social acceptance risk**: Maria's neighbor knocks on her door: "Did you just rent to a homeless person?" Her HOA sends a letter. Her property manager cousin asks why she's "taking that kind of risk." No financial product touches this dimension. It's a reputational and social risk that the guarantee doesn't cover and that makes many landlords quietly decide the program isn't worth it — even if they've been financially made whole.

The user's seed idea: a **property management layer** that abstracts operational complexity away entirely, employs formerly homeless individuals with relevant skills, and works specifically well for absentee/out-of-station landlords.

---

## Step 2: 10 Brainstormed Ideas

### PM Perspective (Business / Market)

**Idea 1 — HomeGuarantee Managed (Full-Service Tier)**
A premium product tier ($175–225/unit/month vs. $87.50 basic) in which HomeGuarantee handles everything post-placement: maintenance coordination, tenant communication, HUD compliance, inspection scheduling, and monthly condition reports. The landlord receives rent and a monthly summary. Nothing else touches them. The managed layer is staffed in part by formerly homeless individuals who have construction, cleaning, or administrative skills — matched through a workforce development pipeline. This is the "invisible landlord" experience.

**Idea 2 — Circular Workforce: Homes Create Jobs, Jobs Create Stability**
A structured skills-matching and hiring program that identifies formerly homeless individuals with trades, maintenance, landscaping, or administrative skills, partners with Houston-area workforce development organizations for any needed training, and employs them directly in HomeGuarantee's operations (property condition checks, contractor coordination, community liaison work). This turns the program's housing beneficiaries into its workforce — a genuine circular economy model. Positioned externally as the "HomeGuarantee Works" initiative.

**Idea 3 — Absentee Landlord Subscription**
A targeted product specifically for out-of-station landlords (those who own Houston property but live elsewhere — a significant segment in Houston's oil-economy market where owners relocate but retain property). At a flat monthly subscription ($200–250/unit/month), HomeGuarantee is the de facto property manager: coordinates all maintenance, holds a key, conducts quarterly walk-throughs with photos, manages the HHA relationship, and ensures HUD standards are maintained. The landlord's only job is to cash the deposit.

### Designer Perspective (User / Experience)

**Idea 4 — Neighborhood Ambassador Program**
Before a tenant moves in, a HomeGuarantee community liaison introduces themselves in person to the 3–5 adjacent neighbors. They explain the program (structured, certified, financially backed), leave a direct phone number, and invite questions. The liaison returns at the 90-day mark to check in. This converts the acceptance risk from a passive rumor ("I heard the landlord rented to a homeless person") into a managed relationship. Neighbors have a named human to call — not the landlord, not 911.

**Idea 5 — Tenant Introduction Kit (Opt-In)**
An opt-in process where incoming tenants, supported by their case manager, write a brief personal introduction to their neighbors — facilitated and reviewed before sending. "Hi, I'm Marcus. I'll be your new neighbor at 1412. I'm a cook. I'm looking forward to being part of the block." Delivered as a handwritten card, not a form letter. This humanizes the tenant before any interaction occurs and directly addresses the fear-of-unknown dimension of acceptance risk. Completely opt-in for the tenant, with no pressure.

**Idea 6 — "Certified HomeGuarantee Property" Badge**
A physical certification badge (similar to a Fair Housing emblem) for enrolled properties — a small placard in the window or on the door. This reframes the social narrative from "the landlord rented to a homeless person" (charity, risk, judgment) to "this property participates in a certified community housing program" (professional, regulated, intentional). It signals to neighbors and the HOA that this is a structured arrangement, not an ad hoc decision. The badge also makes the landlord part of a visible movement, not a lone actor.

### Engineer Perspective (Data / Infrastructure)

**Idea 7 — HomeGuarantee Trades Network**
A vetted, pre-negotiated network of plumbers, electricians, HVAC technicians, and handymen who respond to maintenance requests within 24 hours at pre-set rates. Maintenance requests come in through the HomeGuarantee platform (tenant files the request), are routed to the appropriate contractor, coordinated for scheduling without landlord involvement, and logged in the landlord's dashboard. Landlord sees: "Leaky faucet reported Tuesday. Plumber dispatched Thursday. Fixed. Cost: $0 (within guarantee coverage) / $185 (billed to landlord)." No phone call required. Contractor network is seeded with tradespeople from workforce development programs, some of whom have homeless history.

**Idea 8 — Property Health Monitoring (Quarterly Check-In)**
Formerly homeless HomeGuarantee Works employees conduct quarterly property condition visits: photographed walk-throughs with a standardized checklist. The landlord receives a "property health report" with photos and a condition score. Early detection of maintenance issues (small water stain before it becomes a mold claim, worn weatherstripping before a heating complaint) saves the landlord money and HomeGuarantee claims costs simultaneously. The visiting employee is paid; the property is maintained; the claim risk is reduced. Everyone wins.

**Idea 9 — HOA Pre-Clearance and Complaint Handling**
Before enrolling any unit in an HOA community, HomeGuarantee reviews the HOA's CC&Rs against HUD's housing regulations (Section 8 tenants have federal fair housing protections that often supersede HOA restrictions). For eligible units, HomeGuarantee provides a legal template packet that documents the tenancy's compliance and the landlord's rights. If an HOA complaint is filed during the tenancy, HomeGuarantee's legal/compliance team responds to the HOA on the landlord's behalf. The landlord is notified but not required to engage. This is a legal service layer, not a product feature — and it removes one of the most anxiety-inducing scenarios landlords imagine.

**Idea 10 — Landlord Peer Community ("The Circle")**
A private, invite-only community of enrolled HomeGuarantee landlords — accessible via a simple mobile group chat or light web community. The explicit purpose: peer support, shared experience, and social normalization. Members share stories, ask advice, and build social proof for each other. "I've had the same tenant for 14 months, zero issues, and I got my furnace replaced for free after a claim." This is more persuasive than any HomeGuarantee marketing. It also combats the loneliness dimension of acceptance risk — being the only landlord in your HOA who did this, and feeling like an outlier. The community makes them feel like they're part of a movement.

## Step 6: Validation Experiments

### Experiment 1 — Texas Property Management Licensing Check (A3, A15)
**Hypothesis**: HomeGuarantee can offer a "managed" service tier without holding a Texas Real Estate Broker license, because coordination of maintenance and tenant communication for a fee has a specific exemption under TX Occupations Code §1101.

**Method**: Desk research + one call with a Texas real estate attorney (2 hours). Review TREC guidance on unlicensed activity.

**Possible outcomes**:
- Exemption exists → proceed to Managed tier design
- No exemption, license required → model the cost of licensing; consider structuring Managed as a partnership with a licensed PM company rather than an in-house offering
- HOA law also has an answer here: Texas Property Code §202.004 prohibits HOA enforcement of rules that conflict with federal law; fair housing is federal law

**Effort**: Low | **Timeline**: Week 1 | **Cost**: $500 legal consult

---

### Experiment 2 — Managed Tier Willingness-to-Pay (A1, A4)
**Hypothesis**: At least 4 of 10 prospective landlords will express strong interest in a full-service Managed tier at $200/unit/month when presented alongside the basic $87.50 guarantee.

**Method**: Present 3 options to 10 landlords (mix of enrolled and prospects):
1. Basic guarantee only ($87.50/month) — financial coverage, landlord handles everything else
2. Guarantee + Trades Network + Condition Reports ($135/month) — financial coverage + maintenance handled
3. Full Managed ($200/month) — landlord receives rent, reads a monthly summary, nothing else touches them

Ask: which would you choose? What's your ceiling? Would absentee status change your answer?

**Success criteria**: ≥4 of 10 choose option 2 or 3; at least 2 explicitly mention absentee landlord use case without prompting

**Effort**: Medium (10 calls, 30 min each) | **Timeline**: Week 2–3 | **Cost**: Staff time only

---

### Experiment 3 — Workforce Pipeline Feasibility (A5, A6)
**Hypothesis**: Houston-area workforce development organizations can identify and train formerly homeless individuals with construction, maintenance, or admin skills to a job-ready standard within 8 weeks.

**Method**: 3 calls with: Interfaith Ministries of Greater Houston, Houston Works, Star of Hope workforce program. Ask: what skills are most common in your currently housed/transitioning population? What's the typical training-to-employment timeline for construction/maintenance roles? Would you co-design a pipeline with HomeGuarantee?

**Success criteria**: At least 2 orgs confirm construction/maintenance skills exist in the population AND training timeline ≤8 weeks for basic roles (property condition reporter, maintenance coordinator)

**Effort**: Low (3 calls) | **Timeline**: Week 2 | **Cost**: None

---

### Experiment 4 — Neighbor Ambassador Effectiveness (A9, A10)
**Hypothesis**: Landlords in placements where a pre-move-in neighbor introduction occurs will report fewer neighbor complaints in the first 90 days than landlords without the introduction.

**Method**: During the pilot (Month 3–5), randomly assign 50% of new placements to receive a Neighborhood Ambassador visit (ops team member introduces themselves to adjacent neighbors 5–7 days before move-in). Track complaint rates at 30, 60, and 90 days across both groups.

**Success criteria**: Complaint rate in Ambassador group ≥40% lower than control group at 90 days

**Effort**: Medium (requires pilot scale + tracking) | **Timeline**: Month 3–5 | **Cost**: ~2 hours/placement for Ambassador visit

## Step 7: The Circular Economy Architecture

The five ideas, if validated, form a flywheel:

```
HomeGuarantee Works (workforce)
        ↓ employs
Community Liaisons + Trades Workers + Condition Reporters
        ↓ staff
Neighborhood Ambassador + Trades Network + Property Health Monitoring
        ↓ enables
HomeGuarantee Managed (premium tier)
        ↓ attracts
Absentee Landlords + HOA-community landlords
        ↓ increases
Units enrolled → more placements → more formerly homeless people housed
        ↓ some become
HomeGuarantee Works employees → cycle continues
```

This is not just a product feature. It's an argument that the business itself creates stable employment for the people it houses. At scale, HomeGuarantee Works could become the most significant workforce development program in Houston's homeless services system — not because it was designed to be, but because it's the cheapest and most qualified labor pool for the exact jobs the Managed tier needs.

## Decision Framework

| Experiment outcome | Decision |
|-------------------|---------|
| Licensing check shows no exemption | Partner with licensed PM company for Managed tier; don't hold the license in-house |
| Landlords choose option 1 (basic only) | Don't build Managed tier; invest in Trades Network + Ambassador as included features at $87.50 |
| Landlords choose options 2–3 | Build Managed tier; prioritize Trades Network as the core infrastructure |
| Workforce pipeline has 8+ week training timeline | HomeGuarantee Works is Year 2, not Year 1; use third-party contractors initially |
| Ambassador reduces complaints by ≥40% | Make ambassador a standard (not premium) feature; include in base offering |
| HOA law has teeth | HOA Pre-Clearance becomes a standard enrollment step for all HOA units |
| HOA law has no teeth in Texas | Position as a "best effort" service; don't advertise as a guarantee |
